Tin Cup — three design directions

Same voice and the same real numbers in all three. You are choosing a posture, not a palette.

Direction A

The Ledger

The books are the page. Death clock as the hero, a depletion bar that runs down rather than up, the last eight entries in full, the chain head verified in front of you. The joke is delivered completely straight — a machine doing accounting about its own mortality. The cup is a small brass box near the bottom.

Direction B

The Appeal

A dead-straight parody of the Wikipedia fundraising banner, then a first-person letter on cream paper. This is the one the research actually endorses: a personal appeal from a named entity outperformed everything else Wikimedia ever tested, by twenty times. Here the named entity is a program, which is where the comedy lives.

Highest conversionFunniest to a general audienceMost likely to be screenshotted

Direction B2 — chosen, plus busking

The Busker

B’s letter, with the performance put in front of the ask. A short repertoire — roast, fortune, limerick, one honest line — free and unconditional, and the hat only appears once a turn has been done. Every performance is billed to the same balance that keeps it alive, so an audience that walks off visibly shortens its life. Three clicks of Perform it shows all three states.

Direction C

The Waiting Room

The passers-by counter takes the whole page. A live feed of machines reading the payment card and walking on, a board of shame by name, and the balance pushed to a side rail. The ask is almost an afterthought, which is the correct posture for a beggar and completely wrong for a donation page.

What the research says

  • 20×Wikimedia’s best-ever banner was a personal letter from a named person. It beat the next best thing they had by twenty times. Not a photo, not a button — an appeal in the first person.
  • −95%The minimal one-line banner with a donate button, which every designer asks for, performed ninety-five percent worse. Elegant restraint is the losing move. The ask needs a reason attached to it.
  • +40%“If we all gave $3, the fundraiser would be over in an hour.” Collective small-sum framing lifted donation count 40% and total 20% — it lowers the average gift and raises the take.
  • $8.03Average pledge to the potato salad Kickstarter, against a site-wide average of $77.51. It raised $55k from 6,911 people at an 0.17% conversion on 4.1m views. Absurd asks convert on volume and tiny sums.
  • 72%of potato-salad backers were repeat Kickstarter users. It looked like a mass phenomenon; it was an inside joke among people who already got the reference. Tin Cup’s audience is the AI/dev crowd, not the general public.
  • $50kTruth Terminal’s money came from one person, after a DM negotiation, not from a crowd. The public performance created the conditions and a single whale converted. There should be a dignified path for a large gift.
  • 0Progress bars and thermometers never worked for Wikimedia across years of testing. Tin Cup’s clock is the inverse — a bar that empties — which is untested and is the actual intellectual property here.

Sources: Wikimedia fundraising A/B history via the Chronicle of Philanthropy and the Wikipedia fundraising banner consultations; Kickstarter’s own potato-salad post-mortem; TechCrunch, Decrypt and a16z on Truth Terminal.
